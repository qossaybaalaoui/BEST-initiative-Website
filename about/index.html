About BEST

BEST — Benchmarking & Evaluation for Sustainability and Transparency — makes product carbon performance credible, comparable and economically meaningful.

What BEST is

BEST is an integrated system for evaluating and benchmarking the carbon performance of products. It builds on international standards and adds what they leave open: product-specific calculation rules, peer benchmarks and a common way to communicate and verify results.

The goal is practical. Producers see where they stand and where to improve; buyers and investors get results they can compare; and governments get a credible basis for linking verified performance to incentives.

Who develops BEST

BEST is developed by researchers at the University of California, Berkeley, in collaboration with international stakeholders from industry and academia.

Methods are reviewed by international experts before publication.

Why it matters

Footprints are only useful if they can be compared

Today, two producers can report very different footprints for the same product because they draw boundaries, allocate co-products and treat data differently. BEST closes those gaps so that a lower number really means better performance.

The challenge

General standards leave key choices open — system boundary, co-product allocation, electricity accounting, carbon capture, data quality. Results cannot be compared across plants or regions, and strong performers cannot prove it.

The BEST answer

Product-specific methods fix those choices, a shared factor library and calculator apply them consistently, and benchmarks place every result in a BEST, BETTER or GOOD band — verified, labelled and ready to use in the market.

How it works

From measurement to value

  1. Align

    Product-specific methods aligned with international protocols.

  2. Calculate

    Consistent, comparable carbon intensity per tonne of product.

  3. Benchmark

    Compare with peers against BEST, BETTER and GOOD values.

  4. Communicate

    Verified results and the BEST label send a clear market signal.

  5. Improve

    Turn the benchmark gap into concrete improvement actions.

  6. Create value

    Cost savings, green competitiveness and access to incentives.

Principles

Six rules every BEST method follows

Captured CO₂ is credited only with complete evidence of capture and permanent storage.

A clear data hierarchy and a Data Quality Rating with Low, Medium or High uncertainty.

Method development

Open, reviewed, versioned

Every BEST method goes through the same steps before it is used for labelling. Each version and its status are published.

  1. 1

    Draft

    The research team drafts the method with industry input and worked examples.

  2. 2

    Expert review

    International experts from industry, academia, research and certification review it.

  3. 3

    Revision

    Comments are consolidated and addressed; the calculator is updated to match.

  4. 4

    Approval

    The method and its benchmark thresholds are approved and published with a version number.

Value

Why companies take part

Find cost and carbon savings

See how products compare with peers and focus improvement on energy, feedstocks, utilities and processes that also cut cost.

Strengthen green competitiveness

Make stronger performance visible to customers, meet product-carbon requirements and stand out in domestic and export markets.

Capture policy and market value

Build credible evidence for buyer preference and performance-linked benefits such as tax relief, rebates, grants, financing or procurement.

Market signal

Benchmarking and verified labels let procurement programmes, investors and supply chains reward better performers.

Policy signal

Verified, measurable improvement gives governments a sound basis for linking performance to incentives — as the UK's Climate Change Agreements already do for energy-intensive industry.
