Step by step

How a BEST footprint is calculated

Set the scope

Define the product, the production route and the plant boundary — from raw-material extraction to the factory gate.

Collect data

Gather one continuous year of plant data: feedstocks, energy, utilities, emissions and wastes. Measured data first, then supplier and database data.

Check the balances

Mass, carbon and energy must add up. Any unexplained carbon is counted conservatively as an emission.

Share with co-products

Where the plant also sells co-products, shared emissions are split between them by a fixed rule.

Calculate by source

Each emission source — feedstock, fuels, process, electricity, steam, utilities, transport, waste — is calculated with its own equation.

Rate and report

A data quality rating, an uncertainty class and the BEST tier complete the result, ready for verification.

What is counted

Every emission source, nothing hidden

Select a source to see how it is treated.

Each input carries its cradle-to-gate footprint — ideally the supplier's verified product footprint. Catalysts are always included.

Every metered fuel is counted once, from its energy content and carbon, plus the emissions of producing that fuel.

Flares, vents, leaks and non-CO₂ gases, measured or engineered from plant data. For ethylene, furnace decoking too.

Full life-cycle factor. The location-based result is mandatory; a market-based result is added where contracts are used.

Steam made on site is counted through its fuel; purchased steam gets the supplier's factor or a documented default.

Only what crosses the site fence carries a factor; on-site utilities are counted through their own energy.

Inbound and waste transport legs not already included in a material's footprint.

Each waste stream follows its real treatment route — incineration, landfill or wastewater treatment.

Deducted only for CO₂ permanently stored or verifiably used, with full evidence. The result before capture is always shown.

Key rules

The rules that make results comparable

  1. First avoid allocation: split the process until each part has one product.
  2. Otherwise share emissions by mass.
  3. Use economic value only when there is no physical basis — or when co-product values differ by more than 5:1.

Only co-products that are actually sold share emissions; everything else is classified as waste or emission first.

  • A flow below 1% of the footprint may be left out — judged by its emissions, not its mass.
  • Included materials and energy must still cover at least 97% of the total.
  • Catalysts and precious metals are never cut off.

Every exclusion is listed with its justification, never silently dropped.

  • Location-based result: mandatory, using the delivered grid factor including losses.
  • Market-based result: reported separately when renewable contracts or certificates are used.

So buyers can compare plants on the same basis, and still see the effect of clean-power purchases.

  • Bio-based inputs are tracked stream by stream, with certificates.
  • Biogenic CO₂ and the biogenic carbon stored in the product are reported separately.

The headline footprint is fossil; biogenic carbon is disclosed, never netted as a credit.

  • Captured CO₂ is deducted only when permanently stored or verifiably used.
  • Energy used to capture, compress and transport it stays in the footprint.

The footprint before capture is always reported alongside, so the deduction is visible.

Data quality rating

How good is the data behind a result?

Each input gets a score from 1 (best) to 5. The overall rating is weighted by each input's share of emissions and sets the uncertainty class. Select a score.

Low uncertainty · up to 1.5Medium · up to 3.0High · above 3.0

Score 1 — Measured, site-specific and third-party verified

Direct measurement at the site for the reporting period, verified by an independent body.

Score 2 — Measured, site-specific, not externally verified

Direct measurement or mass balance at the site, reviewed internally.

Score 3 — Supplier-declared footprint

A supplier's product footprint, compliant with a recognised standard and no more than three years old.

Score 4 — Industry-average or database data

Secondary data from an LCA database or an industry average.

Score 5 — Generic defaults or proxies

IPCC default factors, spend-based proxies or estimates — used only when nothing better exists.
